About Sightline

Sightline draws the terrain along the straight path between two points. It reads 2 m elevation data directly from the public ArcticDEM archive, which covers land north of about 60°N. Nothing is stored on a server: your points live only in this page and in its address, which you can copy to share a path.

Visits are counted anonymously with GoatCounter: that the page was opened, from which country and which site led here. No cookies, and your points and settings are never sent.

The verdict is an estimate from a simple, open model: free-space loss, plus terrain loss worked out two ways (knife-edge diffraction over at most three ridges, and the ITU single-ridge method with its allowance for broad, real terrain), taking the larger. It does not know about reflections, trees that have grown since the images were taken, snow, or noise at the receiver. Read the margin as give or take 10 dB.

Good to know

  • The data is a surface model made from satellite images. Buildings and trees are part of the surface, as they stood when the images were taken.
  • Heights are shown above sea level. The archive measures from a smooth reference shape instead, so Sightline corrects it with the EGM96 sea-level model. Expect a metre or so of difference from local maps.
  • The profile is drawn with the Earth's curvature added, so the line of sight is straight. The readout under the chart gives the real ground height.
  • By default the 2 m data is only read where it matters: near both antennas and wherever the ground comes close to a narrow Fresnel zone. Everything else comes from the 10 m data.
  • Red on the chart and the map means the ground is above the line of sight. "Estimate radio link" adds the radio side: pick a band, a radio and an antenna, and it works out whether the link should hold. It also draws the Fresnel zone, the space the signal needs around the line of sight, and marks in amber where the ground comes inside 60% of it ("grazing").
